Ask for the time somewhere else and Raffi shows it above your launchers. No
keyword setup is needed:

```text
time tokyo                → 05:00, Sat 17 Oct   +1 day · Asia/Tokyo · JST UTC+09:00
time in new york          → 16:00               America/New_York · EDT UTC-04:00
now in paris              → 22:00               Europe/Paris · CEST UTC+02:00
15:00 paris to nyc        → 09:00               15:00 Europe/Paris → America/New_York
3pm london in tokyo       → 23:00               15:00 Europe/London → Asia/Tokyo
```

Press Enter to copy the time as shown, or Ctrl+Enter to copy a full timestamp
such as `2026-10-17T05:00:00+09:00`. Copying needs `wl-copy`.

<Aside type="note">
The world clock is only available in native mode and reads the system time
zone database, so the `tzdata` package must be installed. Without it Raffi
shows "Time zone data not found (install tzdata)". It is on by default; set
`addons.world_clock.enabled: false` to turn it off.
</Aside>

## Places

A place is the city part of a time zone name, so `tokyo` finds `Asia/Tokyo`
and `new york` finds `America/New_York`. Case does not matter and spaces stand
in for underscores. The full name, such as `america/new_york`, works too. A few
short names are built in: `nyc`, `sf`, `la`, `uk`, `utc`, `gmt`, plus some
common cities that have no zone of their own, such as `boston`, `seattle`,
`beijing` and `mumbai`.

A fixed offset from UTC is written `gmt+2` or `utc-5`, meaning two hours ahead
of UTC or five hours behind it. The full zone name keeps the zone database's
reversed sign, so `etc/gmt+2` is two hours behind UTC.

When a name matches several zones, for example `eastern`, you get one row per
zone. An unknown place shows nothing, and words that merely start with `time`,
like an app called `timeshift`, are left alone.

## Converting a time

Write the time, the place it is in, then `to` or `in` and the place you want.
Times can be `15:00`, `9:30`, `3pm` or `3:30pm`. The time is taken as today in
the first place.

When the result falls on another day, the row shows the date and how many days
away it is, from two days before to two days after. A time skipped by a
daylight saving change, such as 02:30 on the night clocks go forward, moves
forward to the next valid time and the row says so. A time that happens twice
when clocks go back uses the first one.

The current time is worked out as you type and does not keep ticking while the
query stays the same.
